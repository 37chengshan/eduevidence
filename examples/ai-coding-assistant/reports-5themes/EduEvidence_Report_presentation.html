最强支持结论

允许使用 AI 对学习结果的影响完全取决于使用规则设计：无护栏放任使用带来实证的独立考试伤害（一项高质量随机对照试验），带护栏使用可消除该负效应——这是本证据集中最可靠、与决策最直接相关的政策结论

关键不确定性 / 反例

护栏能否在 C 语言入门场景复现'兼得'效果：E-003 的护栏效应仅在高中数学练习、教师设计提示的单一情境验证过，教师提示质量与护栏效果在设计中混淆，可复制性未分离检验

主要风险

认知脚手架依赖陷阱（Scaffolding Dependency Trap）：过度依赖实时代码补全导致学生自主调试排错、边界测试与底层计算思维出现退化。

研究问题

我们准备在大学一年级 C 语言课程中允许学生使用生成式 AI 编程助手。它到底会不会提高学习效果？应该怎样引入？

目标学习者

教育阶段：大学一年级；专业：计算机科学与技术；先验知识：绝大多数为零基础初学者，未接触过编程或仅有极少量自学经验；对 C 语言语法、编译错误、调试流程均不熟悉；高中阶段数学基础一般，逻辑思维尚在训练中；学习者特征：学习动机与自律性差异大；对 AI 工具好奇心强、接受度高；容易把 AI 给出的答案当作最终结果而不加理解；部分学生英语水平有限，阅读英文报错与文档困难；期末统一考试是硬性评价约束

课程情境

课程：C 语言程序设计；课程类型：必修核心课程；课程周期：一学期（约 16 周），每周 2 学时理论 + 2 学时上机实验，期末含统一机试与笔试

AI 干预

AI 工具：主流生成式 AI 编程助手（如 GitHub Copilot、ChatGPT/Claude 代码生成、Cursor 等），按学生现有可访问工具为准，不强制指定单一品牌；允许使用：试点方案：允许在调试报错、解释概念、代码走读与重构建议等场景使用；禁止直接提交 AI 生成代码作为作业答案；实验课内设置不使用 AI 的独立评测环节；使用频率：每周上机实验期间按需使用，作业阶段受限使用；通过日志或自报记录使用次数与场景；干预周期：与课程同周期 16 周，覆盖整个学期，期末评测对比

比较条件

随机对照或准实验设计：同专业平行班级/同一班级内随机分组。实验组：允许使用 AI 编程助手并按引导性规则使用；对照组：沿用传统教学、禁止使用 AI。两组教学内容、作业量、教师投入保持一致，仅在期末统一机试与笔试中同卷比较；同时用同一份机试题目在期初做前测，控制基线差异

结果构念

主要结果：独立问题解决、代码质量；次要结果：完成时间、记忆保持、知识获得；风险结果：AI 依赖、过度依赖、迁移下降

研究范围

时间范围：2025-2026 学年第一学期（16 周教学周期）；地域：国内普通本科高校（以本校计算机科学与技术专业大一新生为试点）；研究设计：随机对照试验、准实验、观察性研究

决策成功条件

若实验组在期末统一机试与笔试中的独立编程成绩显著不低于对照组（非劣效），且 AI 使用频率与成绩提升呈正相关、依赖风险指标（无 AI 环境下的独立完成任务率）不显著恶化，则可判定引入 AI 编程助手利大于弊，形成可推广的引入规则与配套教学设计；反之则收紧或取消允许政策

05 适用性与教学干预

适用边界、护栏化试点与止损条件

目标人群：国内普通本科高校计算机科学与技术专业大学一年级零基础新生（首次修读 C 语言程序设计，无系统编程经验，学习动机与自律性差异大，期末统一机试与笔试为硬性评价约束）

目标情境：一学期约 16 周必修核心课程，每周 2 学时理论 + 2 学时上机实验；试点干预为允许学生按引导性规则使用主流 GenAI 编程助手（Copilot、ChatGPT/Claude 代码生成、Cursor 等），禁止直接提交 AI 生成代码作为作业答案，实验课内设不使用 AI 的独立评测环节；成功条件为实验组期末无 AI 统一机试与笔试成绩对对照组非劣效，且依赖风险指标不显著恶化

适用于谁：有明确护栏设计的有限规模试点：随机分组（或同班准实验对照）、禁止直接提交 AI 代码、教师设计提示引导使用、每周上机按需使用且记录使用日志、以期末无 AI 统一机试与笔试为唯一学习效果判据（非劣效成功条件）、期中设无 AI 独立小测作为止损点；试点结论可用于指导同校、同课程情境的引入规则制定

不适用于：无护栏放任使用（E-002 实证伤害）；以作业完成率、完成速度等 AI 在场任务表现作为成效宣传或成功判据；将 n=10/n=3 小样本正效应与质性主题上升为'引入 AI 利大于弊'的政策依据；将试点结果直接推广到其他课程（如数据结构、算法）或全校范围；基于 2023-2025 工具代证据推算 2026 工具环境的效应量

适用条件：

  • 护栏必须落地：禁止直接提交 AI 生成代码作为作业答案，引导性使用规则（调试报错、解释概念、代码走读、重构建议），教师参与设计提示——对应 E-003 的护栏条件
  • 无 AI 独立评测体系：期初无 AI 机试前测、期中无 AI 独立小测（止损点）、期末无 AI 统一机试与笔试，评分与监考机制排除 AI 访问
  • 随机对照或准实验设计：同专业平行班/同班随机分组，两组教学内容、作业量、教师投入一致，仅 AI 政策不同；样本量以 Bastani -17% 效应量为参考做功效分析
  • 非劣效成功条件：实验组期末独立成绩显著不低于对照组才判定利大于弊，出现显著负效应即收紧或取消允许政策
  • 过程监控：记录工具版本、使用场景与频率日志，报告各组实际 AI 使用量与对照组依从性（污染检查）
  • 元认知配套：自评 vs 实际成绩对比、AI 辅助前后独立小测对比，识别挣扎者并差异化支持（应对 E-008 能力错觉与 E-009 分化风险）
  • 报告规范：明确退出率、缺失数据处理与意图治疗分析，教师分配与提示设计透明化并跨教师验证护栏可复制性

解释期：提示而非答案（第 5-8 周）

AI 规则：只允许请求“解释/定位/提示”类输出，禁止任何形式请求生成代码或直接答案（这是 Bastani E-003 验证过的护栏核心：不给直接答案，只给引导性提示）。禁止复制粘贴 AI 输出到编辑器；所有 AI 协助产出的代码必须本人重写。每周使用日志记录：工具与版本、提问场景、提示原文、AI 回答要点、本人收获与困惑。

  • 引入 AI 使用，但严格限定为“解释者”角色：用 AI 解释概念（指针是什么、函数调用栈如何工作）、解释报错含义、逐行解释给定代码片段

  • 提问前置要求：学生必须先写出自己的尝试、卡点与假设，再向 AI 提问；AI 输出中若含代码片段，学生须关闭 AI 后自己重新敲入并逐行解释
  • 实验课每周固定 30 分钟无 AI 独立编程环节，与 AI 使用场景交替进行
  • 第 8 周末进行期中无 AI 独立小测（函数、数组、指针，综合题为主），同时教师审查使用日志中的提示质量

协作期：走读与重构（第 9-13 周）

AI 规则：允许 AI 审查、定位、解释、建议重构，但作业代码主体必须本人撰写，AI 生成整段代码直接提交视为违规（按一次警告、两次记零分、三次取消实验组资格处理）。任何 AI 建议采纳时须附解释。每周使用日志与反思持续记录；工具版本（含模型代际）必须登记。

活动：

  • 用 AI 对自己的代码做走读审查（找 bug 位置、指出边界条件疏漏、提出重构建议），但采纳建议必须填写“采纳理由”（为什么正确、为什么采纳、不采纳的理由）
  • 分阶段小项目作业（综合运用数组/指针/结构体，约 150-200 行）：设计 → 手写实现 → AI 走读 → 修订 → 反思五步提交

  • 实验课每周 30 分钟无 AI 独立编程环节继续执行，题目难度随课程推进
  • 对每周无 AI 独立小测排名后 20% 的挣扎者：教师个别辅导，AI 权限降级（退回解释期规则，禁止走读/重构类使用）；排名前 20% 的加速者：维持协作期规则上限，不得提前解锁任何“生成代码”权限
  • 第 13 周末进行第三次无 AI 独立小测（全范围综合题）

结果检查：第三次独立小测 M2 的组间对比（非劣效持续监测）；挣扎者干预组（后 20%）独立小测轨迹是否回升作为分化控制指标；作业相似度检查（防 AI 直接提交）违规数为零。

迁移期：无 AI 独立考核（第 14-16 周 + 第 20 周延迟测试）

AI 规则：完全禁用 AI（与对照组同条件）。本阶段测量的是“移除 AI 后独立完成任务的能力”，即学习效果的唯一有效判据。

活动：

  • 全面撤离 AI：第 14 周起实验组与对照组同样进入无 AI 复习与模拟机试阶段，演练无 AI 环境下的综合编程（模拟期末题型与时长）
  • 期末统一机试（无 AI 机房：断网、禁用一切 AI 工具与终端外接设备，随机题序，代码相似度检测）与统一笔试（概念、读程序、改错题）
  • 期末考试后第 4 周（第 20 周）进行延迟保持测试（独立完成 2 道综合编程题），测量 retention
  • 全组进行期末自评问卷（自估期末机试得分区间），与实测成绩对照，生成能力错觉指标

结果检查：期末机试+笔试：实验组 vs 对照组非劣效检验（主判据，预注册阈值：效应量 d 置信区间下界不低于 -0.2 判定非劣效，显著优于则判定优效）；延迟保持测试组间对比（secondary）；自评-实测偏差幅度（能力错觉指标）组间对比。

停止条件

  • 任一周无 AI 独立小测出现显著负效应（组间 d<-0.2）→ 当周触发护栏审查，确认原因前暂停该周 AI 使用
  • 日志审查发现“直接请求代码”类提示占比连续两周超 10%，或作业相似度检测确认 3 起及以上 AI 代写事件 → 强化护栏（收紧提示模板、增加无 AI 环节时长）并通报全体
  • 期末统一机试/笔试非劣效检验失败（d 置信区间下界 < -0.2）→ 判定试点失败，下学期收紧或取消 AI 允许政策，并出具完整失败报告
  • 期末延迟保持测试显示实验组显著低于对照组 → 即使机试非劣效，也判定 retention 风险成立，政策维持收紧

  • 任一时点出现系统性作弊或监考漏洞（机试现场发现 AI 访问）→ 立即中止该场成绩并重新评估评估机制

效果评价设计与全部可验证来源

研究问题：在大学一年级 C 语言课程中，按引导性规则（护栏）允许学生使用生成式 AI 编程助手，相对于完全禁止使用，是否能在不损害（非劣效于）学生无 AI 独立编程能力的前提下提升学习体验，并控制 AI 依赖、能力错觉等风险？其效果能否在学期级保持与迁移（后续课程）中维持？

基线：第 1-2 周无 AI 环境机试前测：在断网、无 AI 工具（禁用 IDE 插件）的机房中，用与期末机试同构但难度更低的题目（表达式求值、分支/循环基础、简单 I/O、读程序写结果）独立作答，配少量笔试概念题。用途：(1) 组间基线等价性检验（t 检验/标准化均差 SMD < 0.2）；(2) 作为学习增量（后测−基线）的协变量；(3) 分层异质性分析（低/中/高基线组，参照 Kazemitabaar E-005 前测分层结论）。成绩不计入或仅极小权重计入平时分，避免动机污染。

后测：第 15-16 周期末无 AI 统一机试+笔试（两组同卷、同一监考环境）：(1) 机试：断网环境独立完成综合编程题（数组/字符串、函数、指针、结构体），自动评测通过用例（客观正确率）+ 双人盲评代码质量（可读性、结构化、命名、无冗余）；(2) 笔试：概念题、读程序写结果、程序改错、简单算法设计。机试与笔试均为唯一学习效果判据，全程禁止 AI。

保持测试：学期结束后 2-4 周（寒假初或下学期第 1-2 周）无 AI 延迟复测：与期末机试等难度等价的平行卷（同题型、新题目），配笔试要点复测，测量学期级保持而非即时近迁移——回应证据集中仅 Kazemitabaar 有 1 周保持数据（E-005，不显著结果）、Bastani 仅即时测量（E-002/E-003）的空白。

迁移测试：双通道迁移测量：(1) 任务类型迁移——保持测与期末笔试中加入练习中未出现的新任务类型（如从编写迁移到调试与修复给定代码、从算法题迁移到代码补全与阅读解释、从 C 概念迁移到伪代码/类 C 语言问题），检验跨任务泛化（回应 Bastani/Kazemitabaar 迁移距离仅限同领域同题型的局限）；(2) 跨课程迁移——下学期数据结构课程前 4-6 周的无 AI 评测表现（第一次阶段性测验与大作业），作为长期迁移追踪（frame secondary 落实）。

过程指标

  • 上机实验任务完成率与正确率（AI 在场条件下测得，属任务表现，不并入学习结论）
  • 任务完成速度/耗时（对照 Bastani E-001 +48%/+127%、Shihab E-011/E-012 快 35% 量级，仅作教学法线索）
  • AI 使用频率：每周使用次数、会话时长（工具日志+自报交叉验证）
  • AI 使用场景分布：调试报错/概念解释/代码生成/重构建议各占比
  • AI 输出接受率与修改率（接受后是否理解性修改）
  • 求助 AI 前的独立尝试时长与尝试次数（衡量先思考后求助）
  • 作业按时提交率与通过率
  • 对照组 AI 使用量（自报+期末匿名问卷）：污染检查，用于依从性分析
  • 工具版本与型号记录（应对工具代际漂移，回应 methodology tool_version_effect）

学习指标

  • 期末无 AI 机试客观得分（用例通过率/正确率）——主判据，参照 Bastani E-002/E-003 独立考试范式
  • 期末无 AI 机试代码质量人工评分（双人盲评取均）
  • 期末无 AI 笔试得分（概念理解与改错能力）
  • 期中（第 8 周）无 AI 独立小测得分——中期止损依据
  • 学习增量：后测总分 − 基线前测总分（gain score），两组比较
  • 保持测得分（学期后 2-4 周延迟复测）
  • 迁移测得分（新任务类型 + 数据结构课程表现）
  • 以上全部指标均在无 AI 独立评测环境中采集，任何 AI 在场条件下测得的指标一律归入 process_metrics，不进入学习效果结论（task_vs_learning 分离铁律）

风险指标

  • 无 AI 环境独立完成任务率：期中/期末独立评测中完整独立完成（不求助）的任务占比，与基线对比（frame success_condition 核心风险指标）
  • 能力错觉（Prather E-008）：每次独立评测前自评成绩 vs 实际成绩的差值（overconfidence gap），追踪其随学期变化的趋势；AI 辅助任务后的自评 vs 随后的无 AI 独立小测成绩对比
  • AI 依赖度自评量表：离开 AI 时的焦虑/信心、完成任务前必须先问 AI 的倾向（5 点李克特，期中/期末各一次）
  • 过度依赖行为：作业中直接提交未经理解的 AI 生成代码比例（护栏违反率）、AI 输出不经阅读直接采用的次数占比
  • 分化风险（Prather E-009 widening gap）：基线低分组在无 AI 后测中的相对表现 vs 高分组，检验差距是否被扩大
  • 元认知指标：能否用自己的话解释 AI 建议的原理（学期中抽查+期末口试，回应 Shihab E-013 学生不理解建议'如何/为何'工作的担忧）

05 Applicability & Intervention

Applicability boundary, guardrailed pilot, and stop conditions

Target population: Zero-baseline freshmen majoring in Computer Science and Technology at regular undergraduate universities in mainland China (taking C Programming for the first time, with no systematic programming experience, wide variation in motivation and self-discipline, and the unified machine-based and written final exams as hard assessment constraints).

Target context: A one-semester ~16-week compulsory core course with 2 credit hours of theory + 2 hours of lab per week; the pilot intervention allows students to use mainstream GenAI coding assistants (Copilot, ChatGPT/Claude code generation, Cursor, etc.) under guided rules, prohibits submitting AI-generated code directly as homework answers, and includes independent no-AI assessment segments in lab sessions; success requires the treatment group to be non-inferior to the control group on the end-of-term no-AI unified machine-based and written exams, with no significant worsening of dependency risk indicators.

Suitable for: A limited-scale pilot with explicit guardrail design: random assignment (or quasi-experimental control within the same class), prohibition of directly submitting AI code, teacher-designed prompt guidance, on-demand weekly lab use with usage logging, the end-of-term no-AI unified machine-based and written exams as the sole learning-outcome criterion (non-inferiority success condition), and a mid-term no-AI independent quiz as a stop-loss point; pilot conclusions can guide introduction-rule design for the same university and course context

Not suitable for: Unguardrailed, unfettered use (E-002 empirically demonstrates harm); using AI-present task performance such as homework completion rate or completion speed as effectiveness publicity or success criteria; elevating n=10/n=3 small-sample positive effects and qualitative themes to policy basis for 'AI introduction is net beneficial'; directly generalizing pilot results to other courses (e.g., data structures, algorithms) or the whole university; extrapolating effect sizes for the 2026 tool environment from 2023-2025 tool-generation evidence

Conditions:

  • Guardrails must be implemented: prohibit submitting AI-generated code directly as homework answers; guided usage rules (debugging errors, explaining concepts, code walkthroughs, refactoring suggestions); teacher involvement in prompt design — matching E-003's guardrail conditions
  • No-AI independent assessment system: no-AI machine-based pre-test at term start, mid-term no-AI independent quiz (stop-loss point), end-of-term no-AI unified machine-based and written exams, with grading and proctoring mechanisms excluding AI access
  • Randomized controlled or quasi-experimental design: random assignment of parallel classes in the same major / within the same class; identical content, workload, and instructor effort across groups, differing only in the AI policy; sample size determined by a power analysis referencing Bastani's -17% effect size
  • Non-inferiority success condition: only if the treatment group's end-of-term independent scores are not significantly lower than the control group is the introduction judged net beneficial; a significant negative effect triggers tightening or revocation of the allow policy
  • Process monitoring: log tool versions, usage scenarios, and frequency; report each group's actual AI usage and control-group compliance (contamination check)
  • Metacognitive support: comparison of self-assessment vs. actual scores and independent quizzes before/after AI assistance; identify strugglers and provide differentiated support (addressing E-008 illusion of competence and E-009 differentiation risk)
  • Reporting standards: explicit dropout rates, missing-data handling, and intention-to-treat analysis; transparent teacher assignment and prompt design; validate guardrail replicability across teachers

Claims beyond the evidence boundary

  • Claiming guardrails necessarily eliminate the independent-exam negative effect in the C introductory context — the guardrail was only validated in a single context (high-school mathematics, teacher-designed prompts); this adjudication only asserts that 'guardrail conditions are worth testing in the pilot'
  • Claiming learning improvement or retention within the 16-week semester — no semester-level evidence exists; all intervention windows are ≤4 sessions / single sessions / short training periods, and this adjudication does not presume the pilot outcome direction
  • Counting the AI-present task performance gains as learning outcomes — this adjudication explicitly classifies them as task performance, supporting only 'AI present improves task performance'
  • Using the n=3 EFL writing qualitative studies and the n=10 brownfield studies as quantitative policy basis — they serve only as pedagogical design cues
  • Using the Denny review (UNSUPPORTED, abstract-only verification) as quantitative evidence that 'assessment is broken' — this item carries no evidential function
  • Extrapolating 2023-2025 tool-generation effect sizes to GPT-5-class agent environments — this adjudication only flags tool-generation drift risk and requires version logging in the pilot
  • Claiming a direction for cross-course transfer (e.g., subsequent data structures performance) — no transfer measurement evidence exists; the frame's secondary tracking is data yet to be collected
  • Claiming an overall judgment of 'AI introduction is net beneficial' or 'net harmful' — the evidence only supports 'effects depend on usage-rule design'; no directional judgment is made before pilot results

No-generation phase: hand-written fundamentals (weeks 1-4)

AI rule: Generative AI coding assistants are completely banned (Copilot, ChatGPT/Claude code generation, Cursor autocomplete, and all other forms). For errors and conceptual questions, students must first troubleshoot independently or consult the teacher/classmates; the goal is to build syntax sensitivity and the most basic debugging loop under no-AI conditions, forming the foundation for judging AI output correctness in later phases.

Activities:

  • All code written by hand: syntax introduction, variables and expressions, control flow; the full compile-and-run loop completed manually with no AI assistance of any kind
  • Error-reading training: the teacher demonstrates how to read compile error line numbers and hints; students keep a personal 'error log' (original error text + own troubleshooting process + final cause)
  • At the end of week 4, the course's first no-AI independent quiz (variables, branches, loops, 8-10 problems) serves as the semester baseline
  • The teacher explains the course AI policy and the guardrail rules for each subsequent phase; students sign informed consent and usage-log commitments

Outcome check: Week-4 no-AI independent quiz score is baseline B0; the treatment and control groups should show no significant difference (validating random assignment); error-log submission rate and quality enter the process record.

Explanation phase: prompts not answers (weeks 5-8)

AI rule: Only 'explain/locate/hint'-type outputs may be requested; requesting code generation or direct answers in any form is prohibited (this is the guardrail core validated by Bastani E-003: no direct answers, only guiding hints). Copying and pasting AI output into the editor is prohibited; all code produced with AI assistance must be rewritten by the student. Weekly usage logs record: tool and version, question scenario, prompt text, AI answer key points, personal gains and confusion.

Activities:

  • Introduce AI use strictly limited to the 'explainer' role: explain concepts (what a pointer is, how the function call stack works), explain error meanings, and explain given code snippets line by line
  • The teacher designs and provides Socratic prompt templates ('My code reports error Y on line X, I have already checked Z — what could be the cause?' 'Please explain the execution of this while loop, do not give me new code'); students must ask within the template structure
  • Pre-question requirement: students must first write out their own attempt, sticking point, and hypothesis before asking AI; if AI output contains code snippets, students must close AI, retype the code themselves, and explain it line by line
  • Lab sessions include a fixed weekly 30-minute no-AI independent programming segment alternating with AI-use segments

AI rule: AI may review, locate, explain, and suggest refactorings, but the body of homework code must be written by the student; submitting an entire AI-generated code block directly counts as a violation (one warning, zero score on second offense, disqualification from the treatment group on third). Activities:

  • Pair review: students walk through each other's code and compare 'human review' vs. 'AI review' differences to train critical evaluation of AI output

Outcome check: Third independent quiz M2 between-group comparison (continued non-inferiority monitoring); whether the struggler intervention group (bottom 20%) recovers on independent quizzes as a differentiation-control indicator; homework similarity checks (preventing direct AI submission) with zero violations.

06 Evaluation & Sources

Evaluation design and all verifiable sources

Research question: In the first-year C programming course, does allowing students to use generative AI coding assistants under guided rules (guardrails), relative to fully prohibiting use, improve the learning experience without harming (non-inferior to) students' no-AI independent programming ability, while controlling risks such as AI dependency and illusion of competence? Can the effects be maintained in semester-level retention and transfer (subsequent courses)?

Baseline: Weeks 1-2 no-AI machine-based pre-test: in an offline computer room with no AI tools (IDE plugins banned), students independently answer problems isomorphic to the final machine-based exam but of lower difficulty (expression evaluation, branch/loop basics, simple I/O, read-program-write-output), plus a small set of written conceptual questions. Purposes: (1) between-group baseline equivalence testing (t-test / standardized mean difference SMD < 0.2); (2) covariate for learning gain (post-test − baseline); (3) stratified heterogeneity analysis (low/mid/high baseline groups, following Kazemitabaar E-005 pre-test stratification). Scores count at zero or minimal weight toward the usual grade to avoid motivation contamination.

Post test: Weeks 15-16 end-of-term no-AI unified machine-based + written exams (same paper, same proctoring environment for both groups): (1) machine-based: offline environment, independently complete comprehensive programming problems (arrays/strings, functions, pointers, structs); automatic test-case evaluation (objective correctness) + double-blind human grading of code quality (readability, structure, naming, no redundancy); (2) written: conceptual questions, read-program-write-output, program error fixing, simple algorithm design. The machine-based and written exams are the sole learning-outcome criteria, with AI banned throughout.

Retention: 2-4 weeks after term end (early winter break or weeks 1-2 of the next semester) no-AI delayed retest: a parallel paper of equivalent difficulty to the final machine-based exam (same problem types, new problems), plus written key-point retest, measuring semester-level retention rather than immediate near transfer — responding to the gap that only Kazemitabaar has 1-week retention data (E-005, null result) and Bastani has only immediate measurement (E-002/E-003).

Transfer: Two-channel transfer measurement: (1) task-type transfer — the retention test and final written exam include new task types not practiced (e.g., moving from writing to debugging and fixing given code, from algorithm problems to code completion and reading/explanation, from C concepts to pseudocode/C-like-language problems), testing cross-task generalization (responding to the limitation that Bastani/Kazemitabaar transfer distance is limited to the same domain and problem type); (2) cross-course transfer — no-AI assessment performance in the first 4-6 weeks of the next semester's data structures course (first periodic quiz and major assignment) as long-term transfer tracking (implementing the frame's secondary design).

Process metrics

  • Lab task completion rate and correctness (measured under AI-present conditions; task performance, not included in learning conclusions)
  • Task completion speed/duration (benchmarked against Bastani E-001 +48%/+127% and Shihab E-011/E-012 35% faster magnitudes; pedagogical cue only)
  • AI usage frequency: weekly usage count and session duration (tool logs + self-report cross-validated)
  • AI usage scenario distribution: share of debugging errors / concept explanation / code generation / refactoring suggestions
  • AI output acceptance and modification rates (whether adopted suggestions were modified with understanding)
  • Independent attempt duration and attempt count before asking AI (measuring think-first-then-ask)
  • Homework on-time submission rate and pass rate
  • Control-group AI usage (self-report + end-of-term anonymous questionnaire): contamination check for compliance analysis
  • Tool version and model registration (addressing tool-generation drift, responding to methodology tool_version_effect)

Learning metrics

  • End-of-term no-AI machine-based objective score (test-case pass rate/correctness) — primary criterion, following the Bastani E-002/E-003 independent-exam paradigm
  • End-of-term no-AI machine-based code quality human score (double-blind grading averaged)
  • End-of-term no-AI written exam score (conceptual understanding and error-fixing ability)
  • Mid-term (week 8) no-AI independent quiz score — mid-term stop-loss basis
  • Learning gain: post-test total − baseline pre-test total (gain score), compared between groups
  • Retention test score (delayed retest 2-4 weeks after term end)
  • Transfer test scores (new task types + data structures course performance)
  • All of the above are collected in no-AI independent assessment environments; any metric measured under AI-present conditions is classified into process_metrics and never enters learning-outcome conclusions (task_vs_learning separation rule)

Risk metrics

  • No-AI independent task completion rate: share of tasks fully completed independently (without asking) in mid-term/final independent assessments, compared against baseline (the frame success_condition's core risk indicator)
  • Illusion of competence (Prather E-008): self-assessed score vs. actual score difference (overconfidence gap) before each independent assessment, tracked across the semester; self-assessment after AI-assisted tasks vs. subsequent no-AI independent quiz scores
  • AI-dependency self-rating scale: anxiety/confidence when away from AI, tendency to ask AI before attempting tasks (5-point Likert, once at mid-term and once at final)
  • Over-reliance behavior: share of homework containing directly submitted AI-generated code without understanding (guardrail violation rate), share of AI outputs adopted without reading
  • Differentiation risk (Prather E-009 widening gap): relative performance of baseline-low groups on no-AI post-tests vs. high groups, testing whether the gap widens
  • Metacognitive indicator: ability to explain in one's own words how AI suggestions work (mid-term sampling + final oral exam, responding to Shihab E-013 students' not understanding 'how/why' suggestions work)
  • Over-optimism monitoring: combining E-002's mechanism of students not noticing learning impairment, the correlation between self-assessment-actual difference and independent scores enters the risk report

Success threshold: The introduction is judged net beneficial only when ALL of the following hold: (1) primary criterion — treatment group's end-of-term no-AI unified machine-based + written total is non-inferior to the control group (non-inferiority established, margin 0.3 SD; referencing Bastani E-002's unguardrailed -17% significant harm being eliminated under guardrails in E-003, the guardrail group must not show a same-magnitude negative effect); (2) learning gain (post-test − baseline) non-inferior to control; (3) risk indicators not significantly worse — no-AI independent task completion rate not below baseline and not significantly below control; illusion-of-competence difference (self-assessment − actual) not significantly larger than control; (4) retention and transfer tests not significantly worse than control; (5) AI usage frequency and learning gain not significantly negatively correlated (a significant negative correlation triggers a warning, even though the evidence set does not confirm a positive correlation, per the frame success_condition wording). Meeting these conditions yields generalizable introduction rules and accompanying instructional design; otherwise the allow policy is tightened or revoked.

Analysis plan: Design: within-class/parallel-class random assignment (or quasi-experiment with baseline equivalence); sample size via power analysis referencing Bastani's independent-exam -17% effect size (α=0.05, power=0.80, non-inferiority margin 0.3 standard deviations), avoiding n=10-class samples. Primary analysis: between-group non-inferiority test of end-of-term no-AI machine-based + written total scores (one-sided, H0: treatment worse than control ≥ margin), supplemented by ANCOVA (baseline pre-test as covariate) and mixed-effects models (teacher/class as random effects, addressing instructor_effect); learning gain (gain score) compared between groups. Task-learning separation: process_metrics are reported in a separate section explicitly labeled 'AI-present task performance' and never written into 'learning gain' conclusions (responding to task_vs_learning_guard and the E-002 lesson); learning_metrics use only no-AI independent assessment data. Risk analysis: between-group non-inferiority test of independent completion rate; illusion-of-competence difference (self-assessment − actual) between-group and over-time comparison; baseline-stratified heterogeneity analysis (E-005/E-009 differentiation tests). Compliance: control-group contamination check (self-report + anonymous questionnaire + computer-room spot checks), reporting both intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol analyses; report dropout rates and missing data (multiple imputation). Optional extension: add an unguardrailed group (standard AI interface) when resources allow, forming a three-arm design that replicates Bastani's 'unguardrailed -17% / guardrailed -0.004' policy contrast in the C context; transparent guardrail prompt-design process, validating guardrail replicability across ≥2 teacher classes.

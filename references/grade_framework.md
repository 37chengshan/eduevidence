# GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation)

> **GRADE Certainty of Evidence Rating Framework for Social & Educational Science**

---

## 1. Four Levels of Evidence Certainty

1. **High (++++)**: We are very confident that the true effect lies close to that of the estimate of the effect.
2. **Moderate (+++o)**: We are moderately confident in the effect estimate; the true effect is likely to be close, but there is a possibility that it is substantially different.
3. **Low (++oo)**: Our confidence in the effect estimate is limited; the true effect may be substantially different from the estimate.
4. **Very Low (+ooo)**: We have very little confidence in the effect estimate; the true effect is likely to be substantially different from the estimate.

---

## 2. Five Downgrading Criteria (1 to 2 Levels Down per domain)

1. **Risk of Bias (Study Limitations)**: Flawed randomization, allocation concealment failure, lack of blinding in subjective assessments, selective outcome reporting.
2. **Inconsistency (Heterogeneity)**: High unexplained heterogeneity across studies (I^2 > 50%, Cochran Q p < 0.10, conflicting effect directions).
3. **Indirectness**: Population mismatch (e.g. university CS majors vs K-12 primary students), intervention mismatch, surrogate outcomes (task completion instead of delayed transfer).
4. **Imprecision**: Wide 95% confidence intervals crossing clinical/educational decision thresholds, small pooled sample size (N < 200).
5. **Publication Bias**: Funnel plot asymmetry (Egger regression p < 0.10), missing negative trial results, Rosenthal Fail-Safe N < 5k + 10.

---

## 3. Three Upgrading Criteria (Only for Observational/QED without initial downgrades)
1. **Large Effect Size**: g > 0.80 (upgrade 1 level); g > 1.20 (upgrade 2 levels).
2. **Dose-Response Gradient**: Clear monotonic relationship between exposure dose and outcome gain.
3. **Plausible Residual Confounding**: All plausible unmeasured confounders would diminish the observed effect rather than explain it.
